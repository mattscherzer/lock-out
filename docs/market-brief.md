# Market Brief (Partnership and Funding Outreach)

**Read this first.** LockOut is free and has no revenue model that anyone has agreed to. "Sales" here means finding funders, institutional partners and distribution channels. No buyer or funder has been spoken to. Nothing below is a validated claim about what they want.

## One-line pitch

A free, independent, open-source tool that tells Berlin cyclists, in one sentence, whether their parking spot is risky tonight and what lock to use, built on public police data and honest about its limits.

## Why now (with sources to verify)

- 22,630 bike thefts recorded in Berlin in 2025, down 16.5% from the year before (Polizei Berlin PKS Kurzbericht 2025, pp. 5 and 19). Average declared damage was €1,246 per completed case (p. 42).
- Counts are falling, so lead with the cost and the decision, not with a rising-crime story.
- Press reports of a record national average insured claim (about €1,270) and a clearance rate under 5% are unverified. Do not quote them externally until checked.
- Berlin is building secured station parking, so open-street parking will remain the gap.
- A lock maker launched a global theft map in 2026, so the space is attracting attention.

## How we differ (talking points, with caveats)

| Alternative                   | What they do                                    | Our difference                                                           | Careful about                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Official Kriminalitätsatlas   | Counts and rates by Bezirksregion               | Finer areas, time of day, advice                                         | The atlas is official and trusted; do not disparage it                           |
| Berlin Bike Theft Clock       | District-by-time-band shares from the same data | Neighbourhood level, lock advice, activity adjustment, confidence labels | Only true if the data supports the finer level. It is also free and already live |
| Litelok map                   | Global map by a lock maker                      | Independent, Berlin-specific, no product to sell                         | Its Berlin depth is unknown; do not claim it is weak there                       |
| Generic lock advice and tests | Publicly available guidance                     | Place- and time-specific, anchored in the same guidance                  | We reuse the guidance; we are not a replacement                                  |

## Discovery questions for each audience

- "What decision would you make differently with a neighbourhood-level theft signal?"
- "Do you already use the official atlas or any theft map? What is missing?"
- "What evidence would you need to act on it, and who has to sign off?"
- "Would you fund or promote a free tool, and under what conditions?"
- "What would make you distrust it?"

## Likely objections and honest answers

| Objection                                    | Honest answer                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "The police atlas already exists."           | True. The proposition is finer areas, time of day and advice, if the data supports them. That is being tested.                                           |
| "Riders won't check an app."                 | Unproven. A demand test is planned this month.                                                                                                           |
| "Data is under-reported."                    | Yes, by some estimates about half of thefts go unreported (unverified). The product shows its uncertainty; it does not hide it.                                                  |
| "Who is responsible if the advice is wrong?" | Advice follows published police and ADFC guidance, is labelled with confidence, and is reviewed before launch. A formal liability review is still to do. |
| "Why should we pay?"                         | The product is free to riders. Funding would cover development and hosting; no commercial terms exist.                                                   |

## What not to claim

- That riders want it, or that it reduces thefts. Neither is shown.
- That no competitor exists. Several exist, and a closer one went live with 2026 data.
- That the model is "AI-validated" or predictive. No model exists yet.
- That bike thefts are rising. Berlin's fell 16.5% in 2025.
- Any partner, user or pilot numbers. There are none.
- Any revenue or pricing. None is set.

## Outreach sequence

1. Finish the validation phase and write down the results.
2. Pick one audience for the first conversations (my suggestion: ADFC and cycling communities, since they overlap with the rider tests and carry no conflict of interest).
3. Use the demo and the test results, not the pitch, in the first meeting.
4. Record what each audience asks for, and update the personas and the canvas.
