Model

A small area's own numbers are not enough, so it borrows from its neighbours

1 · Few thefts3 thefts in a year says little. It could be chance, like judging a café on 3 reviews.
→
2 · Blend with neighboursWe mix the area's count with its district's, and the district's with the city's. The fewer thefts, the more we lean on the neighbours.
→
3 · Rank into tiersLow for 60% of areas, Medium for 25%, High for 15%. Tiers compare areas with each other.

The method is called Empirical Bayes. "Empirical" means the blend is learned from the data, not set by hand.

AI product decisions

Where we use machine learning, and where we chose not to

Learned from dataA statistical model trained on police data. Its blend strengths are estimated, not guessed. We tested it on future quarters it never saw. We also tried an OpenStreetMap model and left it out because it helped too little.
Deliberately not AINo live model, no chatbot, no LLM. Ratings are computed ahead of time, so the app is fast and works offline. Lock advice comes from vetted guidance, because wrong advice has a real cost.
Honest about uncertaintyEvery answer shows a confidence level and the data date. Thin areas are hatched as limited data. A fairness report checks every district.

Limits

What the data cannot tell us

Hours are "last seen"Only 38% of thefts are found missing within 4 hours, so time of day describes when a bike was last seen.
Time and day are citywide patternsMedian 4.4 and 5 thefts per area and level. Too thin per area, so the card says so.
Reported thefts, not risk per bikeAn OpenStreetMap exposure model gained almost nothing (2.469 vs 2.474) and is not in the MVP.
8 areas have no record84 have fewer than 5 street thefts in the model's window (to 10 Oct). Both are flagged as limited data.
